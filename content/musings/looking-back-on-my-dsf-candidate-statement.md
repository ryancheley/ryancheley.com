In November I posted [details on my DSF Candidate Statement](https://ryancheley.com/2025/11/04/dsf-candidate-statement/). I was ultimately elected to the DSF Board in late November and was voted as the treasurer as I had planned.

I thought now, about 3 months later, might be a good time to do a retrospective on my time on the board.

I have 4 main objectives for my two year term on the board. They are:

1. Getting an Executive Director (ED) to help run the day-to-day operations of the DSF
2. Identifying small to midsized companies for sponsorships
3. Implementing a formal strategic planning process
4. Setting up a fiscal sponsorship program to allow support of initiatives like [Django Commons](https://django-commons.org/)

I set this as a goal for my first 90 days

> ### My First 90 Days as Treasurer
>
> If elected, here's my concrete plan:
>
> **Month 1:**
>
> - Audit current sponsors and revenue sources
> - Identify 20 target companies (mix of sizes) currently using Django
> - Work with current board to draft outreach templates and sponsorship value propositions
>
> **Month 2:**
>
> - Begin systematic outreach to target companies
> - Track response rates and refine approach
> - Engage with Django community leaders to identify additional prospects
>
> **Month 3:**
>
> - Report results to board
> - If we've secured commitments for additional $30K-$50K in annual recurring revenue, propose budget to hire part-time ED
> - Continue to push forward the ED recruitment process

Now, it really is kind of hard to determine, after the fact, when my first month started. The election results were announced in late November and my first board meeting was in December. This is when the transition occurred and I was elected treasurer.

December is a hard month to do anything really. Lots of holidays, lots of year end stuff, and so I'm going to count December as month 0[ref]I mean, Django is a Python web framework, so 🤷🏼[/ref]

The board meets the 2nd Thursday of each month which means that Jan 11, 2026 started month one, officially.

Let's look at my checklist for month 1:

## Audit current sponsors and revenue sources

I haven't done this nearly as well as I would have hoped. I was able to review the GitHub sponsor data but this is only about 1/3 of total source of donations.

Although there is a board assistant, Cathernie, who is amazing and would be able to help me with this, I didn't ask until right before the February meeting

I'll mark this as a 🤦🏻‍♂️

## Identify 20 target companies (mix of sizes) currently using Django

It turns out the Fundraising Working Group had already done all of the work of identifying companies that use Django. All that I had to do was to look through their list and identify some potential companies to outreach to.

I took that list and identified potential companies based on the work that the WG had done, so this a solid ✅

## Work with current board to draft outreach templates and sponsorship value propositions

I didn't do this during the first month either because again, it turns out the Fundraising WG had already done much of the work! I did bring it up to review at the February meeting, so I'll mark this as a solid ✅

Let's look at my checklist for month 2:

## Begin systematic outreach to target companies

Since I didn't have a chance to work with the board to `draft outreach templates and sponsorship value propositions` in my first month this is kind of a hard one to have accomplished. That being said, at the March meeting I did present my template and asked the board for feedback on it. My hope is that before the next meeting I'll have gotten some feedback and have had a chance to outreach to one or more companies.

##  Track response rates and refine approach

Again, this is delayed because of the outreach. You can't really track response rates if you haven't started!

##  Engage with Django community leaders to identify additional prospects

In the Fundraising Working Group Stephanie Goulet [posted](https://django-dsf.slack.com/archives/C0784UYUVN0/p1771955900042619) the text below

```
Hi, lead for $30k Platinum sponsor: PostHog

Why:
posthog posted on the django forum that they're django-based open source product analytics
posthog has already shown interest in sponsoring the django ecosystem, as evidenced by them them currently sponsoring Django Girls for $500/mo ($6k/yr) and  previously sponsoring django rest framework via their 2022 valentines day campaign. what they said about it: "DRF is the framework for building APIs in Django and the foundation of PostHog's web API"
posthog's spending money guidelines include sponsorships
posthog's largest sponsorship is tailwindcss for $2.5k/mo ($30k/yr)
posthog's general philosophy and decisions seem to mesh well with being fair and giving back, e.g.  their values, pricing principles, being open source, etc.
posthog excerpt on sponsorship process:
Request sponsorship
If you know of a project that is fundamentally important to PostHog, add the project to this page via a PR and tag Charles. If we decide to sponsor, we can set up the sponsorship via either Open Collective or GitHub. To get an invite to Open Collective, create an account first with your posthog.com email address and then ask Charles to invite you.
Anyone on the PostHog team can do this!

Possible approaches:
Talk to someone at posthog about them requesting a platinum sponsorship for $30k
One option is the author of the django forum post and co-CEO, James Hawkins: django forum profile, linkedin, posthog profile
If not him, anyone at posthog can request posthog sponsorship for a project that supports posthog, so here are the all the other posthog team member profiles. If anyone knows someone working there, that would be another good option.
and even if they don't get the full company to donate, this is a thing: "In addition to sponsoring key projects, we also provide a $100/month budget for every team member to sponsor projects that have helped them."
Another option is asking more generally through their community question portal

Next steps:
Decide how much to ask for (imo an ask of $30k is appropriate, even if they might ultimately give less...or more :eyes:)
Decide who at PostHog to reach out to (and the method by which you'll reach them, e.g. email, linkedin message, forum message, etc.
Decide who from FWG will reach out
```

This wasn't me engaging with community leaders so much as it was community leaders engaging with/at me which was a good thing!
